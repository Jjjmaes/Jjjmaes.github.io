Three things to check before you download a font

1. The licence. “Free” can mean free for personal use, free for commercial use, or free with conditions such as attribution. Read the licence on the font's page before you use it in a client project. A font that is free to download is not automatically free to use everywhere.

2. The format. For the web, WOFF2 is the smallest and best-supported modern format. TTF and OTF are fine for desktop use and as fallbacks. If a family offers WOFF2, use it as your first choice and keep the others only if you need them.

3. The weights you actually need. Each weight is another file your visitors download. Start with regular and bold; add light or black weights only when the design genuinely needs them. A variable font can cover the whole range in a single file.

Self-hosting in brief

To self-host a font, download the files, put them in your project, and declare them with @font-face in your CSS, pointing src at the WOFF2 file first. That keeps your pages independent of third-party font services and avoids an extra network request on every visit.
